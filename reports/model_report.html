The verdict

Winner: margin ridge, a straight-line point-spread model

Six algorithms finished in a statistical tie at the top. The highest scorer, margin LightGBM, was not significantly better than the simpler linear models (Holm-adjusted p = 0.74). Our rule, written down before the test, says to pick the simplest model in a tie. So the winner is margin ridge: it predicts "Team A by 6.5" from about 40 pregame clues, then a bell curve turns that into a win chance. It is easy to explain, fast, and gives both a point spread and a percentage.

It beats our own Elo rating in 11 of 12 seasons and trails the Vegas closing line by about 0.012 log loss (Vegas knows about injuries, quarterbacks and weather; we don't use them). Its average miss on the final margin is 12.9 points, against 12.4 for Vegas.

Recall

Which winners did it catch?

83.2%of home-team wins caught
63.3%of road-team wins caught
73.3%balanced accuracy (average of both)
15.5%of Vegas upsets called (2,394 upsets)

Recall means: of all the games a side really won, how many did we see coming? Road teams are usually the underdogs, so they are harder to call. Upsets are rare by definition: a well-calibrated model should not call many of them, or it would be wrong much more often. Chasing upset recall makes the overall picks worse.

How we tested

The method, and its limits

  • Time machine, never a shuffle. Tune on 2010–2013 only, then train on all earlier seasons and predict the next one, 2014 through 2025.
  • No peeking. Every clue is computed from games before kickoff. An automated "time-travel" test erases future scores and checks that nothing changes.
  • Both sides. Each game is predicted as A-vs-B and B-vs-A and averaged, so the two chances always add to 100%.
  • Fair fights. Every algorithm sees the same games. Gaps get 95% ranges from a game-week bootstrap and Holm-corrected head-to-head tests.
  • Clean data. 0 duplicates, 0 impossible ties, and 22 of 22 champions match real history.

Known limits

  • No injuries, quarterback changes, weather or betting data go into the model.
  • Bowls and playoff games are close to coin flips for every model (opt-outs and motivation).
  • The committee is people, not a formula; the emulator copies their past habits.
  • The 2026 forecast freezes today's ratings; uncertainty comes from simulated luck and a ±3.1-point "true strength" wobble per team.

Committee emulator weights (standardized): wins +3.6, losses -3.2, rating +2.1, sos +2.0, conf champ -0.0, is power +1.2.

Glossary

Log loss
A penalty score for predictions. Being confident and wrong costs a lot. Lower is better; a coin flip scores 0.693.
Brier score
The average squared gap between the chance we gave and what happened (1 or 0). Lower is better.
Recall
Of the games a side really won, the share we predicted correctly.
Calibration
Whether our percentages mean what they say: 70% should come true about 70% of the time.
Walk-forward test
Train on the past, predict the next season, step forward a year, repeat.
Ridge regression
A straight-line formula with a "seat belt" that stops it from trusting any one clue too much.
